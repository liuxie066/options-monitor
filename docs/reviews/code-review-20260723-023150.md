## Findings

### 1 — fixed — high — analysis could silently use a broader set than readiness approved

- **Path**: readiness segment result -> S4 episode selection -> paired metrics.
- **Failure**: selecting every episode in a mechanically ready segment would
  re-include episodes or horizons that failed fee, point-in-time, outcome-matrix,
  or policy-pair checks. Reimplementing those filters in analysis would create
  a second authority and permit drift.
- **Fix**: S3c readiness emits the exact promotion-usable episode IDs and
  outcome keys. S4 consumes only those keys; all excluded facts remain visible
  in coverage and inconclusive reason counts.
- **Coverage**: readiness eligibility counts, exact paired counts, under-ready
  stop, and optional-facet integration.

### 2 — fixed — high — inferred DTE could move episodes across threshold boundaries

- **Path**: bounded sensitivity -> DTE input -> tier reconstruction.
- **Failure**: deriving DTE from expiration minus `observed_at_utc.date()` is not
  equivalent to the production market/trading-date DTE, especially around US
  UTC boundaries. This could manufacture a threshold result from unavailable
  history.
- **Fix**: capture exact decision-time DTE, capture ratio, and remaining
  annualized return as material episode facts. Sensitivity requires these
  inputs and fails closed for older episodes without them.
- **Coverage**: missing DTE makes all nine scenarios inconclusive for that
  episode; canonical capture retains exact threshold inputs.

### 3 — fixed — medium — P3 could compare different horizons or lose usable replacement evidence

- **Path**: episode outcome selection -> P3 vs P0 comparison.
- **Failure**: terminal-first selection could choose a current-contract terminal
  row without a replacement result even when an earlier mechanically valid
  same-horizon replacement row existed. Falling back independently for P0 and
  P3 would then compare different horizons.
- **Fix**: P3 selects the longest mechanically eligible row that contains a
  usable replacement outcome, and its P0 baseline uses that exact row. Missing
  same-horizon replacement evidence remains inconclusive.
- **Coverage**: P3 switch-vs-hold, switch-vs-close, and transaction cost fixture.

### 4 — fixed — medium — transaction-cost report counted fees but omitted current close spread

- **Path**: decision episode -> policy transaction-cost totals.
- **Failure**: reporting only `decision_close_fee` understated execution cost
  even though counterfactual outcome economics already used the ask price.
- **Fix**: persist `(ask - close_mid) * multiplier * contracts` as decision
  close slippage. Close cost is fee plus that slippage; P3 switch cost adds
  replacement open/exit fees and observed entry slippage. Missing components
  make the cost metric inconclusive rather than zero.
- **Coverage**: exact close and switch cost totals and captured slippage.

### 5 — fixed — medium — review recommendations could be mistaken for executed trades

- **Path**: recommendation projection -> policy net/regret metrics.
- **Failure**: mapping `review` to either close or hold would invent a manual
  decision and bias the policy comparison.
- **Fix**: net incremental results are emitted only for determinate close/hold
  states. Close-attributable regret/benefit is zero for hold/review because no
  close is recommended, with the measurement basis stated explicitly. Review
  is never imputed as an execution.
- **Coverage**: P0 close, P1 review, P2 hold/close, P3 close/switch fixtures and
  paired deltas.
